## Interpretation

**This is a key finding that differs from the tutorial's illustrative expectation.** The missingness in `credit_score` is **not random** — it is concentrated in higher-risk loans. Among loans with a missing credit score, only 36.2% are Current (vs. 62.1% in the full dataset), while Delinquent (23.4% vs. 10.6%) and Default (10.6% vs. 4.5%) are both over-represented.

This means excluding rows with missing `credit_score` from any credit-risk analysis would disproportionately remove higher-risk loans, likely **biasing** average credit scores upward and understating true portfolio risk. Rather than silently dropping these 47 rows, any credit-risk analysis should report the exclusion explicitly and consider whether missingness itself should be treated as a risk signal (e.g., a flag column) rather than simply filled or dropped.

The `loan_purpose` distribution among missing rows looks broadly similar to the full dataset, with no single purpose category standing out as the driver of missingness.
