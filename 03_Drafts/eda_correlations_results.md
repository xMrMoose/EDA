## Interpretation

There is a **strong negative correlation (-0.84)** between `credit_score` and `interest_rate` — well above the ±0.7 "strong" threshold. Higher-credit-score borrowers receive systematically lower interest rates, which is expected for a lending portfolio and confirms the rate-setting process is functioning as designed.

**This is the dominant relationship in the dataset — every other pair of numeric variables is essentially uncorrelated** (all other |correlations| ≤ 0.05), including loan_amount vs. annual_income, which the tutorial suggested checking but which shows no meaningful relationship here (0.02).

In the scatter plot, Default and Delinquent loans (see `outputs/scatter_credit_rate.png`) visibly cluster toward the low-credit-score / high-interest-rate corner of the chart, reinforcing the loan_status grouping results from Step 7: credit score and interest rate together look like a promising input for a future default-risk model. As the tutorial notes, this is a hypothesis worth investigating further, not a proven causal relationship — correlation alone doesn't establish which of the two variables (if either) is driving default risk, since interest rate is set based on credit score at origination.

**Additional observation:** the scatter plot shows a distinctly **banded/stepped pattern** rather than a smooth linear relationship — interest rates cluster into visible tiers roughly aligned with credit score ranges (e.g., ~500–580, ~580–650, ~650–700, ~700–750, ~750+), with rates dropping sharply at each tier boundary rather than declining gradually. This suggests loan pricing may follow a tiered rate-card structure keyed to credit score bands rather than a continuous formula — worth confirming with the lending/pricing team, since it affects how a future rate or risk model should treat credit score (as categorical tiers rather than a continuous input).
